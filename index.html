I am a PhD candidate in Economics at the University of Barcelona and an affiliated researcher at the Institut d'Economia de Barcelona (IEB), supervised by Dirk Foremny and Miguel Almunia. I am a public economist working on inequality, tax design and tax compliance. I am currently working on the incidence of personal and corporate taxes and on how taxation affects firms' automation decisions.

Before my PhD, I worked as a research assistant at the EU Tax Observatory and at the Public Sector and Fiscal Policy Unit of the Banco de España. During my PhD I have been on research visits at the Paris School of Economics (fall 2025) and the Institute for Fiscal Studies (spring 2026).

Research

Publications

Fiscal Drag with Microsimulation: Evidence from Spanish Tax Records with Esteban García-Miralles · Fiscal Studies, 47(2): 113–137, 2026
  • Personal income tax
  • Fiscal drag
  • Inflation
  • Microsimulation
  • Administrative data
Journal article Working paper version Media coverage

Abstract: Fiscal drag arises when nominal tax parameters remain unchanged despite nominal income growth, thereby increasing effective tax rates and revenue. We use Spanish administrative tax records and a detailed microsimulation model to examine fiscal drag in personal income taxation through two complementary approaches. First, we estimate tax-to-base elasticities to assess the progressivity of the tax system and potential fiscal drag under homogeneous income growth. We uncover significant heterogeneity in elasticities across income sources, across the individual income distribution and in the underlying mechanisms. Second, we conduct counterfactual simulations to quantify the actual impact of fiscal drag from 2019 to 2023, finding it accounts for about a third of revenue growth. Our findings offer insights for public finance modelling, revenue forecasting, and tax policy design.
